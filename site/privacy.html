Privacy Policy for DeviceInfo

Last updated: November 13, 2026

DeviceInfo shows information about the Android device it runs on: the system, the hardware, the display, the storage, the battery, the sensors, the cameras, the network state and diagnostics. It is an app for Android phones and an app for Wear OS watches, published by Serhii Zhyhunenko (SerZhyAle). This policy covers both packages and says which one it means where they differ.

In short:

  • The developer receives none of your data. The developer runs no server, keeps no accounts, and the apps contain no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code.
  • The apps cannot reach the internet. Neither package declares the internet permission.
  • What the apps show stays on the device that shows it, unless you copy it, save it, share it, or send it from your watch to your phone.

1. What the apps do not do

  • They have no telemetry: no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no advertising identifier and no third-party tracking library. The components they are built from are listed in section 8.
  • They create no account and ask you to sign in nowhere.
  • They do not ask for your location, contacts, photos, files, microphone, camera or phone number. They list the sensors and the cameras of the device and what they can do; they take no pictures and record no sound.
  • They do not read the data of other apps. The phone app checks one thing about another app: whether Google Play services is installed and which version it is, because the report names it.

2. What the apps read on your device

The apps read, on the device itself, what Android offers to any app: the system version and build, the hardware and the processor, the display, the memory and storage sizes, the battery state, the sensors and camera capabilities, whether the device is online and over which kind of link, and similar diagnostics. Values that identify your device or its place in a network - the signature hash of the app, local network addresses and storage mount paths - are hidden in the copy you make by default, and appear only when you choose to copy the full report and confirm it.

3. What the apps store

  • Phone: which sections of the report are expanded, whether you have answered the question about notifications, and the reports your watch sent to the phone. The reports are files in the app's own storage; the phone keeps only the most recent few and removes older ones.
  • Watch: no report and no account.
  • Android backup is switched off for both packages, so none of this is copied to your Google account. Everything is removed when you uninstall the app.

4. What leaves the device

Only through an action you choose:

  • Copy places the text of the report, or of one section, on the clipboard. Other apps can read the clipboard as Android allows.
  • Save writes the report as a text file into the Downloads folder of the phone.
  • Share hands the text of the report to the app you pick in the Android share sheet. DeviceInfo does not choose the destination and does not see it.
  • Send to phone, on the watch, sends the text of the watch report to your paired phone over the Wearable Data Layer of Google Play services, a link between your own devices. The phone stores the report, shows the notification "Watch report received" and answers with a short acknowledgment. Nothing goes through a server of the developer.

5. Permissions

PackagePermissionWhat it is for
PhoneACCESS_NETWORK_STATETells whether the device is online, on Wi-Fi or mobile data, metered or behind a VPN, for the Network section of the report.
PhonePOST_NOTIFICATIONSShows the notification "Watch report received" when your watch sends a report. The app asks once, with a short explanation, the first time you open it; you can turn it on later from the menu of the System information screen. With notifications off, the phone still keeps the report and the watch tells you that notifications are off.
WatchACCESS_WIFI_STATENames the Wi-Fi bands and standards the radio of the watch supports.

There is nothing else: no internet, location, contacts, storage, camera or microphone permission.

6. Google Play services and Google Play

The link between watch and phone uses the Wearable Data Layer of Google Play services, which Google runs on the devices under its own terms. Google Play, the store, may give the developer aggregated statistics such as installs and crash rates from devices whose owners share them with Google. The developer receives no device-level data from the apps.

7. Children

The apps are not directed at children and collect personal data from no one.

8. Third-party components

The licenses of the components the apps are built from are listed in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt, shipped inside both packages and in the source repository: AndroidX, Material Components, Jetpack Compose and Wear Compose, Dagger and Hilt, Gson, Timber, the Kotlin libraries and Google Play services (Wearable). None of them is an analytics, advertising or tracking library.

9. Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app removes everything it stores, including the reports received from your watch. You can also clear its storage in the Android settings. A file you saved to Downloads, or text you copied or shared, is yours to delete.

10. Open source

DeviceInfo is open source software released under the MIT License. The full source code is publicly available at GitHub.

11. Changes to this policy

When the apps change what they read, store or send, this page changes in the same release and the "Last updated" date at the top moves. The text on this page is the text of the version of the apps that is currently published.
